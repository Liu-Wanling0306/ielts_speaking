01. Describe an interesting conversation you had with a very old personBand 7.5

I'd like to describe a conversation I had with my grandfather when I was a child. It happened around Chinese New Year, when people in my hometown usually set off fireworks and firecrackers. I was actually quite scared of them because they were extremely loud, so I didn't want to join in. My grandfather noticed this and told me the traditional story of Nian, a fierce monster that was said to appear every winter and threaten villagers. According to the story, people eventually discovered that Nian was afraid of loud noises and fire, so they used firecrackers to drive it away. That's why setting off firecrackers became a traditional part of Chinese New Year celebrations. I found the story fascinating because it turned something I was afraid of into something meaningful. After that conversation, I became much less nervous about firecrackers and even joined my family in the celebrations. Looking back, it was a simple conversation, but it helped me understand a traditional custom through a story told by someone I trusted.

记忆节点：爷爷｜春节｜年兽故事

Whenever I think about Chinese New Year now, I still associate the tradition with that conversation with my grandfather, so he remains a particularly memorable older person to me.

03. Describe an impressive course (English lesson) you had and enjoyedBand 7.5

I'd like to talk about a short English lesson I found online. I came across it while scrolling through short videos, and surprisingly, it was only about ten minutes long. The lesson focused on a problem that I have personally experienced as a non-native English speaker: translating everything into Chinese in my head before speaking English. The teacher explained that this makes communication slower and can easily lead to unnatural expressions. What impressed me was that the lesson gave several practical ways to change the habit. For example, I could connect objects around me directly with English words, carry vocabulary cards, and practise shadowing instead of translating sentence by sentence. The teacher also emphasized patience, because changing a language habit takes time. I found the lesson useful because it didn't just give me vocabulary; it changed the way I think about speaking English. Since then, I've tried to respond more directly in English during my IELTS practice. It was short, but it had a surprisingly lasting influence on my learning habits.

记忆节点：10分钟英语课｜翻译习惯｜shadowing

Part 3｜预测题主题 × 个性化高分回答

这里保留你的真实经历作为例子，但答案重点是分析，不是复述 Part 2。

Old people, conversations and generations

1. How can young people learn from old people?
Older people have accumulated practical experience that younger people simply don't have. They can share stories about how they dealt with uncertainty, relationships and difficult decisions. At the same time, young people should not accept every traditional idea uncritically; they can compare that experience with current knowledge.
2. Why do some people prefer talking to older people?
Older people often have more life experience and can offer a different perspective. I also think conversations with them can feel less competitive because they are not necessarily trying to impress you. For example, my grandfather taught me a traditional Chinese story, and that conversation stayed with me for years.
3. Do you think older people and young people can share the same interests?
Definitely. Age doesn't determine every interest. People of different generations can enjoy photography, travel, music or even technology together. The important thing is whether they have something meaningful to talk about.
4. Why should young people respect older people?
Respect is important because older people have contributed to their families and communities and have valuable experience. However, I think respect should be mutual rather than based simply on age.

English learning and teachers

1. What makes a good language teacher?
A good language teacher needs both knowledge and patience. They should explain difficult points clearly, but they should also understand that students make mistakes when they are learning. I particularly value teachers who give practical feedback rather than simply correcting every error.
2. How can people improve their English speaking?
They need regular exposure and active practice. For example, they can use shadowing, speak about familiar topics and try to think directly in English instead of translating everything from their native language. The key is consistency rather than studying intensively for only a few days.
3. Is AI useful for language learning?
Yes, it can provide instant feedback and make practice more accessible. For example, I use AI tools to improve my English expressions and practise IELTS questions. However, teachers still matter because emotional support and human interaction cannot be fully replaced by AI.
4. Can technology replace language teachers?
I don't think so. Technology can provide information and personalized exercises, but teachers can understand a student's emotions, motivation and long-term progress. I see AI as a useful complement rather than a replacement.

Places, nature and beautiful views

1. Why do people like beautiful natural places?
Natural places give people a break from highly artificial and stressful environments. They can also create a sense of scale and calm that is difficult to get from everyday city life. For me, seeing Yulong Snow Mountain after a stressful master's period was particularly refreshing.
2. Why do people take photos of beautiful scenery?
Photos allow people to preserve a moment and share it with others. I also think people take photos because beautiful scenery is temporary. For example, the clouds around the snow mountain were moving quickly, so I wanted to capture the moment before the view changed.
3. Should people protect natural places?
Definitely. Natural places are valuable for both biodiversity and people's well-being. Tourism can bring economic benefits, but if visitors ignore rules, they can damage the environment. So tourism should be managed rather than simply expanded.
4. Is it better to visit natural places alone or with others?
It depends on the purpose. I prefer travelling with a close friend when I want to share an experience, but I also like visiting quiet places alone when I need time to think.

Children and public behaviour

1. Why do some children behave badly in public?
Sometimes they simply haven't learned the appropriate boundaries yet. Parents may also be too busy or too permissive. I think children need clear rules, but they also need adults to explain why a behaviour is inappropriate.
2. How should parents teach children good behaviour?
They should model the behaviour themselves and explain the consequences rather than only punishing children. For example, in places involving animals, parents should teach children not to enter restricted areas simply for a photo.
3. Should schools teach practical behaviour as well as academic knowledge?
Yes, but I think academic learning should remain a priority. Practical skills such as safety, communication and basic responsibility are also important, especially because young people need to function independently in society.

Phones, social media and AI

1. Why do people spend so much time on social media?
One main reason is instant entertainment. After a long day, short videos require very little effort. AI-driven algorithms can also keep recommending content that matches people's interests, so people may intend to check their phones for a few minutes but end up spending hours.
2. How can people avoid wasting time on their phones?
I think setting clear limits is useful, but the bigger issue is developing awareness. Turning off unnecessary notifications and keeping the phone away during focused work can help. Personally, I try not to use short-video apps when I need to concentrate.
3. Can smartphones be useful for important tasks?
Absolutely. I recently used my phone to monitor emails from potential PhD supervisors overseas. Some messages could affect my future academic plans, so in that situation checking my phone was useful rather than simply distracting.
4. Will AI reduce educational inequality?
It has the potential to because students can get information and personalized feedback more easily. However, access to technology is still uneven, and some students may not have reliable Internet or may not know how to use AI effectively. So AI alone cannot remove the education gap.

Decisions, advice and disagreements

1. Why are some decisions difficult to make?
Important decisions often involve uncertainty and competing priorities. For example, choosing between a stable job and a PhD meant balancing financial security with my interest in research. There wasn't a completely risk-free option.
2. Do people need advice when making major decisions?
Advice can be useful, especially when it comes from someone who understands your situation. However, people ultimately have to live with their own decisions, so advice should support judgement rather than replace it.
3. Why do family members sometimes disagree?
They often have different priorities because they come from different generations. My parents valued stability and financial security, while I placed more importance on research and long-term personal fulfilment. Understanding the reason behind the disagreement made it easier to communicate.
4. How can people solve disagreements?
The most useful approach is to discuss the underlying concern rather than trying to prove who is right. A calm conversation can reveal that two people may have different priorities but still care about the same outcome.

Clothes and first impressions

1. Why do some people care a lot about clothes?
Clothes can communicate personality and are often part of a person's first impression. Some people also see fashion as a form of self-expression. However, I don't think appearance necessarily reflects someone's abilities or personality.
2. Can clothes influence people's opinions?
Definitely, especially when people meet for the first time. I once expected my supervisor to dress formally, but he often wore casual T-shirts. Later I realized that his casual appearance had little to do with how rigorous he was as a researcher.
3. Should people wear formal clothes at work?
It depends on the workplace. Formal clothes can be appropriate for interviews or important meetings, but in many research environments, comfort and practicality may matter more. The important thing is to respect the context.
4. Why do people follow fashion trends?
Trends make it easier for people to know what is considered stylish, and social media spreads them very quickly. Some people also follow trends because they want to feel part of a group.

Second-hand shopping and consumption

1. Why do people buy second-hand items?
The main reason is usually price. Good-quality second-hand products can be much cheaper than new ones. Another reason is sustainability because reusing products extends their life and reduces unnecessary waste.
2. Is buying second-hand risky?
It can be. Buyers may not know the exact condition of an item, and online transactions can involve scams. When I bought a camera lens on Xianyu, I asked the seller for a video and carefully checked the details before paying.
3. How can second-hand websites improve their services?
They could provide stronger identity verification, clearer product-condition standards and better buyer protection. These measures would make people more willing to buy expensive second-hand products.

Music, singers and competitions

1. Why do people like music?
Music can regulate emotions and create a sense of connection. People can listen to it when they want to relax, concentrate or remember a particular period of their lives. For me, Sun Yanzi's music was especially comforting when I felt uncertain about the future.
2. Why do people like live concerts?
A live concert provides a shared experience that recordings cannot fully reproduce. Singing with friends and thousands of other people creates a strong emotional atmosphere.
3. Are competitions useful for learning skills?
Yes, if the focus is not only on winning. A competition gives people a concrete goal and encourages them to compare their work with others. For example, I'd like to enter a photography competition to improve my skills rather than simply to win a prize.
4. Should children be encouraged to enter competitions?
They can be, but adults should avoid putting too much pressure on results. Competitions are useful when they encourage practice, confidence and learning rather than making children feel that their worth depends on winning.

Events and celebration

1. Why do people celebrate important events?
Celebrations mark transitions and give people a chance to share important moments with others. My master's graduation was meaningful because it marked the end of a difficult period and the beginning of a new stage.
2. Are traditional celebrations still important?
Yes, because they connect people with cultural traditions and family memories. For example, the story of Nian helped me understand why firecrackers are associated with Chinese New Year.
3. How have celebrations changed?
They have become more personalized and sometimes more influenced by social media. People may share photos and videos online, but I think the core purpose remains the same: spending meaningful time with other people.
